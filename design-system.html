The order book, read like a patient monitor.

Colour carries one meaning only: how urgently a number needs attention. It follows the alarm convention used on medical devices, which the people who make and sell airway products already read without thinking.

Principles

  1. Lead with a sentence. The first thing the CEO reads is a plain-language verdict built from the numbers, not a chart.
  2. Colour is priority, never decoration. Red means act now, yellow means watch, cyan means information, green means resolved. If nothing needs attention, the page is mostly grey.
  3. Panels are divided by rules, not floated as cards. Square panels, soft controls, round chips: radius tells you what you can touch.
  4. One typeface, chosen for legibility. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next was designed by the Braille Institute; its figures don't get confused, which matters for a page of numbers.
  5. Every figure has an action nearby: a list to chase, a file to export, a date to set.
